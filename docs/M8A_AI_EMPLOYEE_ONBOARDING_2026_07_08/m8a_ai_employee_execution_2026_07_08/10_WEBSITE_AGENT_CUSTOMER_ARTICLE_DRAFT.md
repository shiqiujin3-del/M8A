# HK Series Edge Banding Solutions for Skeleton Doors, Narrow Strips and Profiled Panels

For many door and furniture factories, standard edge banding equipment works well on regular boards but becomes difficult when the workpiece is narrow, shaped, light, flexible or uneven. Skeleton doors, decorative door strips, profiled panels and narrow wood components often require more stable feeding, better pressure control and a machine structure designed for non-standard edge banding work.

The HK series edge banding machines are designed for these production challenges. They help factories process skeleton doors, narrow strips and profiled components with more consistent edge quality, cleaner glue application and smoother daily operation.

## Why Skeleton Doors and Narrow Strips Are Difficult to Edge Band

Skeleton doors and narrow wood strips are not as easy to process as standard panels. In real production, factories often face problems such as unstable feeding, uneven pressure, visible glue lines, edge tape deviation and inconsistent trimming quality.

These problems can slow down production and increase rework. When the workpiece is narrow or irregular, manual operation also becomes less stable, especially when factories need repeatable quality for batch orders.

## Common Problems in Door and Furniture Factories

Factories producing doors, cabinet parts and decorative components often need to handle:

- Skeleton door frames
- Narrow wood strips
- Decorative door lines
- Profiled panels
- Custom furniture components
- Small-batch special-shaped products

For these applications, the machine must keep the workpiece stable during feeding and pressing. If the material shifts during operation, the final edge quality can be affected.

## How the HK Series Helps Improve Edge Banding Stability

The HK series is built to support more stable processing for special workpieces. Its structure helps the operator control feeding, pressure and edge application more effectively, reducing the risk of edge tape misalignment and glue inconsistency.
