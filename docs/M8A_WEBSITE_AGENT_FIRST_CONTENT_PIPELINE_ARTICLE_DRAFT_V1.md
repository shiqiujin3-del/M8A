# HK620 Skeleton Door Strip Edge Banding Solution

HK620 is designed for factories that need a more continuous process for skeleton door strip preparation, combining edge banding, grooving, and cutting logic into a clearer production sequence.

As door styles and furniture components become more detailed, many factories are looking for better ways to produce decorative strips, skeleton door strips, and grooved profile components. The challenge is not only whether a single process can be completed, but whether the entire production sequence can remain stable from material feeding to final cutting.

For many production teams, short strip processing creates a practical bottleneck. If strips are cut too early and then sent to edge banding, the workpiece may be harder to feed, press, align, and control. This can increase manual handling and make the result depend too much on operator experience. HK620 addresses this problem through a process-first approach: complete edge banding while the material is still suitable for stable feeding, then move into grooving and final cutting.

## Why Skeleton Door Strip Processing Needs a Clear Sequence

Skeleton door strips and decorative door profiles usually require consistent edge quality, controlled groove positions, and repeatable preparation before assembly. When the work is divided across disconnected steps, every transfer can introduce variation. Repeated positioning, manual trimming, and separate cutting operations can also slow down production planning.

The confirmed process logic for HK620 is simple: edge banding first, grooving second, cutting after that. This sequence helps the factory organize the process around the material condition instead of forcing operators to solve short-piece handling problems later. The result is a more understandable and manageable production route for teams evaluating skeleton door strip manufacturing.

## Typical Factory Pain Points

- Short pieces are difficult to feed and press consistently on ordinary edge banding equipment.
- Separate processes increase handling, alignment checks, and operator dependency.
- Door factories need new process options as product styles move toward more decorative and structured designs.
- Production teams want a clearer path from strip preparation to grooving and cutting.
- Managers need a process that can be reviewed, repeated, and improved before larger-volume production.
